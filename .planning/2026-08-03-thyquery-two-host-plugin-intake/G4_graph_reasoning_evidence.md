# G4 — graph-structured reasoning/control evidence

## Scope and decision target

- This lane tests whether graph-structured reasoning or control has a demonstrated advantage over chain, tree, loop, search, debate, self-refine, evaluator–optimizer, or agent-workflow baselines under comparable inference/compute budgets. `directly_supported`
- The product boundary is the approved SK_v9_B thin, Plan-first Codex/Claude-native plugin: no daemon, wrapper, remote service, parallel execution layer, or second native plan. `directly_supported`
- The decision target is narrower than “can graphs work?”: it is whether published evidence warrants making a reasoning graph the primary ThyQuery control topology rather than retaining Ralph-style intent resolution and one native plan. `directly_supported`
- Search stopped at 16 original peer-reviewed/preprint studies because all relevant evidence classes below were represented and later candidates repeated the same confounds: added calls/tokens, task-specific prompts, offline topology optimization, or model training. `directly_supported`

## Evidence tags

- `directly_supported`: an exact result, method, limitation, or artifact stated in a primary paper or its official repository. `directly_supported`
- `contradicts_premise`: primary evidence directly inconsistent with a general claim that graph reasoning is better. `directly_supported`
- `near_match_only`: graph evidence is real, but its mechanism or deployment boundary does not match a thin hosted plugin. `directly_supported`
- `insufficient`: the source does not expose enough information for the claimed comparison or transfer. `directly_supported`

## Taxonomy: do not collapse these graph families

| Family | What is a node/edge? | How topology is obtained | Closest comparator | ThyQuery relevance | Evidence status |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Runtime reasoning graph | Intermediate natural-language thoughts/states; edges are generation, scoring, refinement, merge, or dependency operations | Hand-authored static graph or dynamically expanded graph during inference | CoT, self-consistency, ToT, iterative refinement | Mechanistically closest to a graph-primary reasoning controller | `directly_supported` |
| Path-aggregation graph | Multiple sampled CoT paths merged by equivalent expressions/answers and verified on a graph | Graph induced after sampling, then a learned or rule-based verifier aggregates | Self-consistency or voting verifier using the same sampled paths | Relevant only if ThyQuery samples many paths and pays that budget | `directly_supported` |
| Trained internal reasoning graph | Graph serialization, attention mask, or graph neural layer inside a trained/fine-tuned model | Training data, SFT/RL, learned pruning, or architectural modification | CoT/ToT under the trained model family | Not implementable as a thin prompt/plugin controller without model training or internals | `near_match_only` |
| Multi-agent/workflow graph | Agents/operators are nodes; messages/control/data dependencies are edges | Offline MCTS, RL, diffusion, mask learning, or fixed topology | Debate, self-refine, manual workflow, single agent | Useful as architecture evidence, but violates or exceeds the approved no-orchestrator/no-remote-service boundary | `near_match_only` |
| Knowledge/retrieval graph | Entities/documents/facts are nodes and semantic relations are edges | Retrieval/index construction | Vector/RAG retrieval | Outside this lane; it does not test reasoning-control topology | `directly_supported` |
| Program/dataflow graph | Code blocks/tools/tasks are nodes and typed dependencies are edges | Compiler, planner, or workflow author | Sequential program/plan | May be a representation schema, but representation alone is not evidence of reasoning-quality gain | `directly_supported` |

## Cross-study answer to the hypothesis

- No included study demonstrates a broad graph-primary advantage while holding model, prompts, tools, training, sample count, model calls, tokens, latency, and offline optimization compute all constant. `insufficient`
- The strongest runtime evidence is task-conditional: graph aggregation helps when a problem has an externally valid decomposition and merge operator, especially sorting/set/document transformations. `directly_supported`
- The strongest close counterexample is ToP’s replication, where a simpler tree beats GoT by 40, 19, and 5 accuracy points on the three GoT task families. `contradicts_premise`
- The cleanest path-aggregation ablation adds only 0.1–0.6 points over a non-graph voting verifier and loses 3 points in the small GPT-4 check. `contradicts_premise`
- Static graph complexity can harm performance: CoT/ToT wins appear in document merging, sorting, MathBench, GPQA/QA exact-match, tree multi-agent tasks, and pruned-workflow cases. `contradicts_premise`
- Dynamic routing/topology systems can improve accuracy per output token, but the successful systems rely on learned routers, offline search, proxy models, multiple agents, or model training. `near_match_only`
- Reported uncertainty is sparse: only a minority of studies show standard deviations, and none of the close runtime-controller comparisons supplies a preregistered power analysis or broad significance testing. `directly_supported`
- Leakage/contamination is generally unmeasured for proprietary or broadly trained models; shuffled options, held-out splits, or author assertions do not establish benchmark non-exposure. `insufficient`
- Cost accounting is inconsistent: many papers omit calls/tokens, and workflow-search papers often report execution cost separately from discovery/training cost. `directly_supported`
- Scaling failures recur as extra calls, latency, memory/KV-cache incompatibility, topology-search cost, proxy error, diminishing returns after a few agents, and sensitivity to graph thresholds or task-specific operators. `directly_supported`

## Exact transfer limits to ThyQuery SK_v9_B

### Candidate A — graph-primary controller

- A graph-primary ThyQuery controller is not supported by matched-compute evidence across general research/planning tasks. `insufficient`
- The closest favorable runtime studies assume task-specific decomposition, automated correctness checks, repeated sampling, and explicit merge operators that ThyQuery’s open-ended intake/research tasks do not reliably provide. `near_match_only`
- Making graphs primary would add a second control structure around the host’s native plan unless the graph were purely a passive representation; that conflicts with the approved one-native-plan boundary. `directly_supported`
- Decision for Candidate A: `NO_GRAPH_BENEFIT_SHOWN`. `contradicts_premise`

### Candidate B — hierarchical outer graph with Ralph subloops

- Published dynamic DAG systems closest to this candidate use a trained router, heterogeneous models, up to tens of nodes, or multiple agents; those mechanisms are not available inside the approved thin plugin boundary. `near_match_only`
- A manually fixed outer graph would inherit the static-graph task-tuning confound identified by S7 and the failure modes shown by S3/S5/S9. `contradicts_premise`
- Candidate B could be reconsidered only if a bounded local benchmark shows higher completion quality at equal host calls/tokens/latency and with the same prompts/tools; no reviewed source supplies that product-specific result. `insufficient`

### Candidate C — Ralph loop plus graph shadow/evaluator

- A passive graph shadow that records dependencies, alternatives, unresolved constraints, and evidence lineage is compatible only if it neither schedules execution nor creates a second authoritative plan. `directly_supported`
- The literature supports graph representations as useful observability, pruning, or merge scaffolds, but it does not show that the representation itself improves reasoning without extra training/search/aggregation. `near_match_only`
- If Candidate C is tested, the graph must be treated as an experimental telemetry/evaluation artifact, not as a claimed performance feature. `directly_supported`
- Minimum fair evaluation: same tasks, model, system/user prompts, tools, temperature, stopping rules, maximum calls, input/output tokens, wall time, and retry policy; report paired task outcomes plus all compute and failures. `directly_supported`
- Required negative controls: Ralph without graph shadow, graph shadow generated but hidden from control, shuffled/irrelevant edges, and a simpler checklist/tree representation with the same token budget. `directly_supported`
- Predeclare graph-benefit success as a practically meaningful paired quality gain with no material increase in failure rate and an explicit cost/latency envelope; otherwise retain Ralph-only control. `directly_supported`

## Contradictions and unresolved unknowns

- “Graph” is not a stable treatment across the literature: it can mean a static prompt program, dynamically expanded thought DAG, post-hoc path merge, attention mask, graph neural representation, or multi-agent communication network. `directly_supported`
- Graph-positive papers often compare against weak or untuned baselines, while replications/ablations show trees, CoT, or a voting verifier can match or beat the graph. `contradicts_premise`
- LLM-judge gains in AGoT conflict with exact-match/F1 results on the same QA families, so judge-based superiority is not robustly established. `contradicts_premise`
- GoT-R1’s graph-structure reward produces no reported GSM8K accuracy increment in its component ablation, so its total improvement cannot be attributed to graph structure. `contradicts_premise`
